# Atlas Demo Script

## Segment 1 - Cold Open On The Problem (0:00-0:15)

**SCREEN:** Atlas professor dashboard, already logged in as Prashant Shah. Cursor moves toward the ECON-241 pricing assignment.

**VO (Prashant):** "94% of students now use AI on coursework. Professors cannot see the learning that happens inside that work. So the submitted artifact proves less every semester, and detection tools punish the wrong problem. This is Atlas: the LMS rebuilt around the reasoning journey."

## Segment 2 - Professor Programs The AI Contract (0:15-0:55)

**SCREEN:** Open "Pricing Strategy Session: The Streaming Problem." Show the AI-expected stance, guided hint depth, "do not give direct answers" instruction, personal-record side notes on, and the journey rubric criteria: framing, grounding, iteration, final design.

**VO (Prashant):** "I am the professor. For this assignment, AI is expected, so I set the terms. Students are designing a pricing strategy for VidStack after a flat price hike caused churn. The AI can ask questions, point them back to elasticity and price discrimination, and challenge weak designs. It cannot hand them the pricing table. And the rubric does not just grade the final table. It grades how the student frames the problem, uses the course concepts, revises under pressure, and defends the final design."

## Segment 3 - Student Works Inside The Contract (0:55-1:45)

**SCREEN:** Student account for Aman Dutta. He opens the pricing session. The assignment terms are visible. He types: "can you just design the pricing tiers for me?"

**SCREEN:** Atlas refuses by citing the professor contract, then asks: "Before pricing anything, who left after VidStack raised the price, and which group is more elastic?"

**SCREEN:** Show Aman initially mixing up elasticity and slope. Then Atlas surfaces the side note: "Elasticity cost you 8 of 12 points on Exam 1." Aman corrects himself and revises toward a basic ad tier, a standard tier, and a premium fence.

**VO (Aman):** "This is my student account. I can see the exact terms my professor set. So watch what happens when I ask for the answer."

*(Let the refusal render for roughly two seconds.)*

"Atlas will not do the work for me. Not because of a generic filter, but because my professor defined what help means here. It asks me to identify the customer segments, catches that I am confusing slope with elasticity, and brings in my own Exam 1 weakness at the moment it matters. Copying from ChatGPT earns me nothing here, because the pricing table is not the only evidence. My reasoning is."

## Segment 4 - The Journey Is The Submission (1:45-2:35)

**SCREEN:** Professor view. Open Aman's journey report. Show transcript evidence pinned to rubric criteria: framing, grounding, iteration, final design. Then switch to the class rollup: "14 of 31 sessions show elasticity confused with slope." Show the draft practice set tied to elasticity and price discrimination.

**VO (Prashant):** "On my side, the submission is not just a PDF or a final answer. It is the session. I can see the student's argument, the moments where the AI pushed, and the exact lines that support the grade. Then Atlas rolls it up across the class. Fourteen of thirty-one students confused elasticity with slope. The strongest recoveries happened after the cannibalization challenge. That tells me what Thursday's class needs to be, and the practice set is already drafted from the same evidence."

## Segment 5 - Close (2:35-3:00)

**SCREEN:** Pull back to the dashboard. End on Atlas mark, both founders, and "Live now - Piloting at Princeton, Fall 2026."

**VO (Aman):** "Every LMS stores teaching. None of them can see learning until now. I am Aman, a physics student at Princeton who works alongside professors every week."

**VO (Prashant):** "And I am Prashant. We built Atlas because we live both sides of this problem. Learning is the reasoning, not the artifact. Atlas is where you see it."

## Production Notes

1. Record screen first, voice second. Capture clean product footage, then narrate against the edit.
2. Use the seeded ECON-241 world only: Prashant Shah, Aman Dutta, VidStack, elasticity, price discrimination, and the 14-of-31 rollup.
3. The refusal must be real. Try to get Atlas to generate the pricing table; the screen should show it refusing by contract and redirecting into the rubric.
4. The side note should be visible and source-backed: "Elasticity cost you 8 of 12 points on Exam 1."
5. The professor rollup should show evidence, not vibes: pinned transcript lines, criterion grades, and the class pattern.
6. Keep the final video at 3:00. If over, cut from Segment 1 or the close, not from Segments 2-4.
