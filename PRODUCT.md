# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Atlas is for professors who need to shape and run real courses, and for students studying inside those courses. The professor is usually working between planning, revision, assessment, and term rollover. The student is trying to understand what matters now and what to do next.

## Product Purpose

Atlas is an LMS organized around course intent: objectives, materials, assessments, and learning activity share one model. The product succeeds when a professor can move from course intent to a usable course draft quickly, review changes with confidence, and students can study from the actual course record without guessing what will be assessed.

Practice grows from the same record. A professor can define a base dataset or problem frame once; Atlas can turn that base into aligned variants so students get more practice without the professor hand-authoring every prompt.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, capable. The product should feel like an expert workspace, not a pitch deck, AI wrapper, or tutorial overlay. Its atmosphere is quiet generative art: small data fields, pixel traces, and course-map marks that calm the surface when noticed and disappear when the work needs attention.

## Anti-references

Avoid AI slop, generic SaaS cards, bland dashboards, anxious trust language, exposed implementation details, feature narration, decorative badges, too many simultaneous buttons, and UI copy that explains what the interface should make obvious.

## Design Principles

- Assume competence: users are smart; the UI should reveal state and affordance without over-explaining itself.
- Show the work: make course structure, coverage, draft state, and next decisions visible through the view itself.
- Keep control quiet: review and approval should be natural workflow states, not repeated reassurance copy.
- Lean is rigorous: solve the real problem directly; avoid speculative layers, fallback mazes, and controls that exist only because the implementation is uncertain.
- Reduce surface area: each screen should have a clear primary job, with secondary actions held back until useful.
- Ship the product, not the scaffolding: hide implementation names, demo labels, and architecture unless they are necessary for the task.
- Atmosphere supports focus: visual identity should sit behind the task, never compete with the course record.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, visible focus states, keyboard-accessible controls, readable touch targets, and reduced-motion safe transitions. Dense product surfaces should stay scannable on desktop and collapse without clipping on mobile.
